# The JjinJhoo Manifesto

**眞主 Manifesto · v1.0**  
*Let us be sovereign in our own lives and make the world a more interesting place, together.*

> **Version:** 1.0  
> **First public draft:** 2026-10-24  
> **Status:** An open manifesto — a first version to read, question, and develop together

> **Translator's note:** *JjinJhoo* (진주, 眞主) refers here to a person who recognizes their own agency and takes responsibility for their choices. It does **not** mean having absolute control over life or death. This English edition is a translation of the [Korean v1.0 text](MANIFESTO.ko.md).

## Declaration

We did not choose to be born, nor can we decide every detail of when or how we will die.

Yet we are alive, and knowing that one day we will die, we can choose how to live now.

To be truly sovereign in life and death does not mean dominating or controlling everything.

It means recognizing our own existence, thinking and choosing for ourselves, and taking responsibility for those choices.

We call such a person **JjinJhoo (진주, 眞主)**.

JjinJhoo is not a title bestowed upon a select few.

Anyone can live as JjinJhoo in their own life. Some already do; others have yet to discover that possibility.

We hope to see them meet and broaden one another's worlds.

And we ourselves are simply one among countless JjinJhoo.

## 01. We Are Each Sovereign in Our Own Lives

No one can live another person's life for them.

We may consider the expectations of others and the standards of society, but we do not mistake them for the final answer to how we should live.

Sovereignty does not mean being able to do whatever we please.

It means taking responsibility for our attitudes and choices even amid circumstances beyond our control.

## 02. We Explore Ourselves

Who am I?

Am I nothing more than my thoughts and feelings?

Is what I want truly what I want?

We do not stop asking these questions.

We question not only to find answers, but because questioning helps us see ourselves and the world anew.

Philosophy does not belong to a select few.

Anyone can be a philosopher who explores themselves and the world.

## 03. We Respect Our Differences

The sea holds countless waves.

They differ in height, shape, and direction, yet not one is entirely separate from the sea.

We, too, live different lives.

We remain open to the possibility that we are connected as one, without imposing that belief on anyone.

Difference is not a problem to overcome.

**Diversity is what makes new experiences and possibilities possible.**

## 04. We Acknowledge What We Do Not Know

We do not know with certainty why the universe exists, what consciousness is, or what—if anything—comes after death.

We respect science, explore philosophy, and remain open to possibilities not yet understood.

We respect experiences without treating any interpretation as absolute.

Belief is free, but we do not impose belief as fact.

We value sharing our questions as much as sharing our convictions.

## 05. We Enjoy Life

Life does not have to produce great achievements to be meaningful.

We can contemplate, love, travel, make music, exercise, and create things.

Some may find their deepest joy in quiet moments of doing nothing at all.

We do not measure human worth by productivity alone.

**Life is not merely a task in which we must prove ourselves; it is also an opportunity to experience.**

## 06. We Support One Another in Every Way We Can

Inner freedom alone is not always enough to live freely.

We may need time, financial stability, knowledge and skills, relationships, and opportunities.

We share what we have, as much as we reasonably can.

Those with financial means can share resources; those with knowledge can share knowledge; those with experience can share experience.

Help is not charity bestowed from above, and support is not something exchanged for obedience.

We do not seek to increase the number of people dependent on others. We hope to see **more people able to choose for themselves**.

## 07. We Meet and Connect

A person can live a whole life on their own.

But when different people meet, possibilities emerge that none could have imagined alone.

Philosophers meet artists, developers meet musicians, and people with long experience meet those just beginning to ask questions.

The roles of teacher and learner are never fixed.

Someone asking questions today may share a new insight tomorrow.

We broaden one another's perspectives, create things together, and sometimes simply enjoy spending time together.

## 08. There Is No Hierarchy Among Us

No one has a monopoly on truth.

No one has the right to rank another person's level of consciousness or worth.

We do not seek to gather under a single answer, a single leader, or a single worldview.

Participation is voluntary, and so is leaving.

We can be together without agreeing with one another.

Receiving someone's help does not oblige us to follow them.

Rather than building up a central figure's authority, we strengthen each person's sovereignty and the connections between people.

**We seek relationships and shared activities that endure even when any one person's influence is gone.**

## 09. We Use Technology for Human Freedom

AI and automation can reduce human labor and open new possibilities.

We hope those possibilities will not be concentrated in the wealth and power of a few, but will help more people live freely.

The purpose of technology is not only to make people endlessly more productive.

It can also help us ease the burdens of survival, discover what we love, and explore and create new things.

We seek to use technology to expand each person's possibilities.

## 10. We Do Not Demand Perfection

People make mistakes, waver, and sometimes become caught up in desire and fear.

We do not see these human experiences only as shameful flaws.

We observe ourselves, seek understanding, and change what needs to change.

We do not demand perfection from one another, either.

Wholeness may not mean being without flaws; it may mean embracing life and the world, including our own imperfections.

We do not dream of gathering people who are already complete.

**We value the possibility of growing together while each of us lives a different life.**

## The World We Hope For

A world where more people live as sovereigns of their own lives and in the face of death.

A world where we can enjoy conversations with people who think differently.

A world where survival does not consume all our time.

A world where everyone can discover what they love and live in their own way.

A world where we enjoy our freedom while respecting the freedom of others.

A world where we meet, learn, help, and create new things together.

In that world, we do not wish to be special.

Nor do we wish to stand above anyone else.

We simply wish to live as one among countless JjinJhoo.

Like one wave among countless others, together forming the sea.

## Final Declaration

**I am my own true sovereign.**

**You, too, are your own true sovereign.**

Because we are different, we can experience more; because we are connected, we can create more possibilities.

No one needs to stand above anyone else.

Nor must we all walk the same path.

We can each live the life we choose, meeting from time to time, helping one another, and enjoying the journey together.

Every meeting enriches our stories, and every connection expands what is possible.

**That is how the world can become a little more interesting.**

---

**生死眞主 — True Sovereignty in Life and Death**  
*A world where each of us is one among countless JjinJhoo.*

## On Versions and Participation

This document is **The JjinJhoo Manifesto v1.0**. It is not a final truth or a doctrine to follow. It is the first ball of thought tossed into the world, inviting people with different lives to connect while respecting one another's sovereignty.

The manifesto itself is open to questions, criticism, and other perspectives. Future versions may be developed through discussion with other JjinJhoo. No one holds exclusive authority to interpret it.

**Each person's sovereignty comes before the manifesto.**
